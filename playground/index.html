Part 3 3D Modeling & 3D Printing — Turning Imagination Into Reality

▶

From digital models to physical objects: what 3D printing can do for psychology research.

Step 1 — What Can 3D Printing Do in Psychology Research?▶

3D printing is already being used across psychology and neuroscience — from tangible brain models for teaching to personalized therapeutic tools. Here are some real examples:

🧠 Neuroanatomy Education

3D-printed brain models help students build spatial understanding of brain structures — a key barrier in neuroanatomy learning. Studies show improved accuracy and satisfaction compared to textbook-only approaches. (Aridan et al., 2024; Minear et al., 2022)

🏃 Youth Behavior Change

3D-printed models of physical activity data helped adolescents reflect on their exercise habits, build identity around healthy behaviors, and spark peer conversations. (IJERPH, 2023)

Read paper →

🩺 Prenatal Attachment

An RCT found that 3D-printed fetal models significantly improved prenatal attachment and reduced anxiety and depression in expectant parents. (JOGNN, 2023)

Read paper →

🧓 Reminiscence Therapy

3D-printed objects from older adults' pasts — cars, bicycles, pets — served as powerful memory cues in reminiscence therapy, adding multisensory depth beyond photographs. (J. Applied Gerontology, 2024)

Read paper →

🤝 Inclusive Classrooms

3D printing as a cooperative learning tool improved peer communication and knowledge retention for students with and without disabilities. (Intl. J. Disability, Dev. & Education, 2024)

🦾 Pediatric Prosthetics

Custom 3D-printed myoelectric prostheses with interactive theming improved psychological well-being in children. Personalization matters. (2024)
