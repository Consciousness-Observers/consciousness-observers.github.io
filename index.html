Consciousness Observers

CO-LAB · Linking Across Boundaries

A student-led, interdisciplinary team working on consciousness — physics, psychology, neuroscience, biomedical engineering and philosophy, in the same room.

What we are

We are a small group of students who want to understand consciousness, and who got tired of having the same conversation in different departments. Physics, psychology, life sciences, biomedical engineering, philosophy — between us we cover most of the disciplines the question touches, and most of the programmes the university has already built for it.

The name says what we are after: Consciousness Observers — Linking Across Boundaries. Learning that crosses boundaries, and a shared way in to working on the question.

How we work

Decentralised and equal. Every member takes part in decisions and in the work of keeping the group running; there are no ranks and no one is anyone’s assistant. We stay deliberately small so that everyone can take part deeply rather than marginally.

We read original papers together, argue about them, and then try to turn what we read into something other people can use — a workshop, a record, a page like this one.

Where we are

CO-LAB was admitted to the Future Learning Center, Zhejiang University as a resident team in 2026. We have our own room in the centre, and we work with what the centre offers — its spaces, its 3D printing and its network of student teams — to run reading groups, workshops and teaching that would be harder to run from a corridor.

It also gives us a place to keep things: the papers we are reading, the questions we are stuck on, the material a workshop needs. A room that can hold yesterday’s whiteboard makes a difference to work that takes more than one evening.

At the same time we are an independent, cross-school student organisation. We are not attached to a single department and we are not run by faculty; the members decide what we do.

What we do

Reading groups. Concepts and Approaches in Psychology takes one concept per session and goes back to the original papers that built it. Five sessions a term, evenings, over tea; the records are kept as an open book.

Workshops. 3D Printing and Digital Brain Anatomy runs from evolutionary origins to a printed model — interactive atlases, real MRI data, virtual dissection.

Seminars and writing. Members present their own work across disciplinary lines, and what is worth keeping gets written up in public rather than on someone’s laptop.
