## Overview

This full-day workshop traces a continuous arc from evolutionary history to digital fabrication. Participants begin with the deep evolutionary origins of the brain, explore its three-dimensional architecture through interactive virtual tools, convert real MRI data into digital 3D models, and ultimately perform a virtual brain dissection — producing a physical, 3D-printed piece they can hold in their hands.

The workshop bridges psychology, neuroscience, MRI image processing, and 3D modeling. No prior experience in any of these areas is assumed.

## What Makes This Workshop Different

**A narrative arc, not a collection of modules.** Rather than presenting four disconnected sessions, the workshop follows a single narrative thread: where the brain comes from → what it looks like in three dimensions → how we capture it with imaging → how we bring it back into the physical world. Each segment builds directly on the last, and participants experience the full translational pipeline from biological tissue to digital model to tangible object.

**Active exploration over passive listening.** Every segment is built around direct interaction: navigating a real 3D brain atlas, manipulating MRI files, and performing virtual dissection. Participants learn by doing, not by watching slides.

**Demystifying neuroimaging.** For students outside radiology or neuroscience, MRI data can feel like a black box. A core goal of this workshop is to show that these data are real, accessible, and personally manipulable — a shift in self-efficacy as much as in technical skill.

**A physical artifact as a memory anchor.** The 3D-printed brain model that each participant takes home is more than a souvenir. It serves as a lasting reminder of what they learned and, we hope, a conversation piece that extends the workshop's impact beyond a single day.

## Session Outline

The workshop proceeds through four parts:

1. **Origins — where brains come from.** An evolutionary perspective on the brain, from simple nervous systems to the human cortex. A narrative introduction that builds curiosity and emotional connection before diving into anatomy.

2. **Structure — navigating the brain in 3D.** Using interactive 3D brain atlases, participants explore cortical lobes, subcortical structures, and the cerebellum. Problem-set-driven exploration replaces passive memorization — students discover the spatial logic of brain organization by navigating it themselves.

3. **Imaging — from scanner to digital model.** An introduction to structural MRI data: what the files look like, how to navigate them, and how to convert them into 3D-printable formats. The goal is to make neuroimaging data feel tangible and tractable.

4. **Dissection — virtual anatomy, physical output.** Using 3D modeling software, participants perform a guided virtual dissection of the brain model generated in the previous session. Free exploration precedes precise, anatomically-informed cutting. Finished models are 3D-printed in color for participants to keep.

A roundtable lunch is embedded between the second and third sessions, designed as an informal but structured space for cross-disciplinary conversation among participants and instructors.
