## Overview

This full-day workshop traces a continuous arc from evolutionary history to digital fabrication. Participants explore the brain's three-dimensional architecture through interactive virtual tools, convert real MRI data into digital 3D models, and perform a guided virtual dissection — producing a personalized brain model they can take home.

The workshop bridges psychology, neuroscience, MRI image processing, and 3D modeling. Throughout the day, participants follow a single narrative thread: where brains come from → what they look like in three dimensions → how we capture them with imaging → how we bring them back into the physical world.

## Session Outline

**Part 1 — Origins.** An evolutionary journey from simple nervous systems to the human brain, opening up a sense of scale and curiosity before diving into anatomy. Rather than starting with dry terminology, this session builds emotional connection to the subject through evolutionary narrative and stories from the history of science, then anchors the discussion in the contemporary context of brain research.

**Part 2 — Structure.** Participants explore the brain in three dimensions using interactive digital atlases, developing an intuitive grasp of cortical and subcortical organization. Working in small groups, they identify candidate brain regions of interest (ROIs) connected to functions they find compelling — memory, emotion, movement, visual awareness — and evaluate how each ROI might translate into a 3D modeling target later in the day. The session transforms passive anatomy review into active, question-driven exploration, giving each group a concrete stake in the afternoon's work.

**Part 3 — Roundtable.** Instructors and participants break into small groups over lunch for open, unstructured conversation. No fixed agenda, no hierarchy — just an opportunity for cross-disciplinary exchange in an informal setting.

**Part 4 — Imaging.** Participants work hands-on with structural MRI data, learning to navigate brain images and convert them into 3D-printable digital models. The session is designed to demystify neuroimaging: by the end, participants have moved from opening a file to generating a model they can manipulate, cutting through the "black box" feeling that often surrounds MRI processing for non-specialists.

**Part 5 — Dissection & Printing.** Participants perform a virtual dissection of the brain model generated in Part 4, guided by the ROI selections they made in Part 2. Free exploration comes first — building spatial confidence — followed by anatomically-informed precise cutting. A live 3D printer runs alongside, producing a full-brain print so participants can observe the fabrication process in real time. Individual brain region models can be printed afterwards.

## Design Principles

*Narrative coherence over modular isolation.* Each segment builds directly on the last. Participants experience the full pipeline — from biological tissue to digital model to physical object — as a single integrated arc rather than disconnected sessions.

*Active exploration over passive listening.* Every segment centers on direct interaction: navigating a 3D atlas, manipulating MRI files, performing virtual dissection. Learning happens through doing.

*A physical artifact as lasting memory.* The printed brain model serves as a tangible anchor for the experience — a reminder of what was learned and a conversation piece that extends the workshop's impact beyond a single day.
